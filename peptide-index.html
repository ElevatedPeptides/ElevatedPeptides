Metabolic & GLP-1 Research

Dual Agonist In Stock

Tirzepatide

Dual GLP-1 & GIP receptor agonist

Tirzepatide is a synthetic dual agonist that activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors — two incretin hormone pathways involved in glucose regulation and appetite signaling. It is FDA-approved as Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (chronic weight management); the dosing below mirrors that approved human label, not a self-derived research figure. Elimination half-life is ~5 days, which underlies the once-weekly dosing interval.
FDA-Approved Drug

GLP-1 Agonist Reference Only

Semaglutide

Single-pathway GLP-1 receptor agonist

Semaglutide is a single-pathway GLP-1 receptor agonist studied for its effects on insulin secretion, gastric emptying, and appetite regulation. It shares the same receptor target as tirzepatide's GLP-1 component but without GIP or glucagon activity. It is FDA-approved as Ozempic/Rybelsus (type 2 diabetes) and Wegovy (chronic weight management); half-life is roughly one week.
FDA-Approved Drug

GH Fragment Reference Only

AOD-9604

Modified hGH fragment (176–191), lipolysis research

AOD-9604 is a modified fragment of the C-terminus of human growth hormone, engineered to isolate the fat-metabolizing (lipolytic) region of the GH molecule without the growth-promoting or insulin-resistance effects associated with full-length GH. Its most important research fact: Metabolic Pharmaceuticals' 24-week Phase 2b obesity trial (536 subjects) failed to show statistically significant weight loss over placebo, and the compound was never advanced to Phase 3 or submitted for FDA approval — development was terminated in 2007. It is currently an FDA Category 2 bulk substance, meaning compounding it is restricted pending further FDA review.
Failed Trial — No Approval

🧪 Suggested Research Dosage

Note300 mcg – 500 mcg/day is the figure most widely circulated in community protocols, but this was not the dose shown effective in the compound's own failed obesity trial — treat this as an unverified community convention, not a validated research dose
FrequencyOnce daily, subcutaneous, commonly referenced fasted upon waking
Sources: AOD-9604 FDA regulatory history

Myostatin Inhibitor Reference Only

Follistatin-344

Muscle-growth & myostatin-inhibition research

Follistatin-344 is a naturally occurring protein that binds and inhibits myostatin and related growth-differentiation factors that normally limit muscle growth. Important distinction: the published human clinical evidence for follistatin in muscle disease (Mendell et al., Nationwide Children's Hospital, NCT02354781 and related trials) used an AAV1 gene-therapy vector (AAV1.CMV.huFS344) injected directly into muscle to make the body produce its own follistatin over time — a fundamentally different delivery mechanism from a recombinant Follistatin-344 protein injected directly, which is what is sold under this name in the research-peptide market. The gene-therapy trial results cannot be assumed to apply to injectable Follistatin-344 protein, which has no published human trial data of its own.
No Human Trial Data (as sold) — Distinct From Gene Therapy

NoteResearch literature references highly variable dosing; no widely standardized community protocol exists — consult the primary literature closely

Mitochondrial In Stock

MOTS-c

Metabolic regulation & longevity research

MOTS-c is a mitochondrial-derived peptide encoded within the mitochondrial genome, studied for its role as a metabolic regulator. The endogenous biology is well documented — human studies show MOTS-c rises roughly 12-fold in skeletal muscle during acute exercise, and higher circulating levels correlate with muscle mass and power output. However, injectable MOTS-c as a therapeutic has no completed human efficacy trials; a Phase 2a trial in prediabetes/obesity (NCT07505745) is newly recruiting. Treat the dosing below as unverified.
Endogenous Biology Studied — Injectable Trials Still Early

Extended half-life GHRH analog

Selank

Anxiolytic & nootropic research, standalone

Selank is a synthetic analog of the endogenous immunomodulatory peptide tuftsin, approved in Russia in 2009 for generalized anxiety disorder, studied for anxiolytic and nootropic effects without the sedation associated with traditional anxiolytic drug classes. Like Semax, it has not been reviewed or approved by the FDA or EMA and is studied almost exclusively via intranasal administration in the available literature.

Sleep-Regulating Nonapeptide Reference Only

DSIP

Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide

DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide) is a nonapeptide first isolated from rabbit cerebral venous blood in 1974 by the Schoenenberger-Monnier group. Real human clinical trials followed in the late 1970s–1980s (small studies of chronic insomniacs, mostly using slow intravenous administration, not subcutaneous), with reported improvements in sleep duration and quality. Pharmaceutical development stalled after the 1990s and it was never FDA-approved; the subcutaneous nightly dosing circulated in community protocols today is not the route or dose used in the original human trials.
