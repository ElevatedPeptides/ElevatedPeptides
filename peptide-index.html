Metabolic & GLP-1 Research

Dual Agonist In Stock

Tirzepatide

Dual GLP-1 & GIP receptor agonist

Tirzepatide is a synthetic dual agonist that activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors — two incretin hormone pathways involved in glucose regulation and appetite signaling. Research models have studied dual-receptor activation for its effects on insulin secretion, gastric emptying, and energy balance, making it one of the most actively researched compounds in metabolic and obesity-related literature. Approximate elimination half-life referenced in pharmacokinetic literature: ~5 days, which underlies the once-weekly dosing interval studied in trials.

🧪 Suggested Research Dosage

Typical Range2.5 mg – 15 mg per dose
TitrationStart 2.5 mg; increase in 2.5 mg steps every 4 weeks as studied, up to a 15 mg ceiling referenced in trials
FrequencyOnce weekly, subcutaneous

Retatrutide

GLP-1 · GIP · Glucagon receptor agonist

GLP-1 Agonist Reference Only

Semaglutide

Single-pathway GLP-1 receptor agonist

Semaglutide is a single-pathway GLP-1 receptor agonist studied for its effects on insulin secretion, gastric emptying, and appetite regulation. It shares the same receptor target as tirzepatide's GLP-1 component but without GIP or glucagon activity, and carries an extended half-life of roughly one week that underlies the once-weekly dosing intervals referenced in the literature.

🧪 Suggested Research Dosage

Typical Range0.25 mg – 2.4 mg per dose
TitrationStart 0.25 mg for 4 weeks, roughly doubling every 4 weeks as studied, up to a 2.4 mg ceiling
FrequencyOnce weekly, subcutaneous

Liraglutide is an earlier-generation GLP-1 receptor agonist studied for the same incretin pathway as semaglutide and tirzepatide, but with a much shorter half-life (~13 hours), requiring daily rather than weekly dosing in the research literature it has been studied under.

GH Fragment Reference Only

AOD-9604

Modified hGH fragment (176–191), lipolysis research

AOD-9604 is a modified fragment of the C-terminus of human growth hormone, engineered to isolate the fat-metabolizing (lipolytic) region of the GH molecule without the growth-promoting or insulin-resistance effects associated with full-length GH. Research has focused on its potential role in fat metabolism studies independent of the GH/IGF-1 axis.

Growth Hormone Axis Research

GH Secretagogue In Stock

CJC-1295 (No DAC) / Ipamorelin

Pulsatile GH secretion research blend

This combination pairs CJC-1295 without DAC (also referenced as Mod GRF 1-29), a growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog studied for promoting a natural pulse of growth hormone release, with Ipamorelin, a selective growth hormone secretagogue known for stimulating pulsatile GH release without significantly affecting cortisol or prolactin. Unlike the DAC version, No-DAC CJC-1295 has a short half-life (referenced at roughly 30 minutes), so research protocols favor multiple smaller daily doses over the DAC version's extended once-weekly profile. Together, they are commonly studied for their synergistic effect on the growth hormone axis.

GHRH Analog Reference Only

CJC-1295 (with DAC)

Extended half-life GHRH analog

CJC-1295 with DAC (Drug Affinity Complex) is a modified GHRH analog engineered to bind serum albumin, extending its half-life to roughly one week — in contrast to the No-DAC version's ~30-minute half-life. This extended profile has made it a subject of research into sustained, steady-state elevation of growth hormone levels rather than the sharper pulses associated with No-DAC protocols.
